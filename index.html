02 / Cosmic field guide

How the universe fits together.

The Solar System is only a small neighbourhood inside the Milky Way. Light, gravity, chemistry and time help astronomers investigate worlds that no spacecraft can visit directly.

☉

The Sun

The Sun is a star powered by nuclear fusion in its core. Its gravity dominates the Solar System, while its light and solar wind affect planets, moons and spacecraft.

Explore the Sun ↗
01

Planet formation

Planets formed from a disk of gas and dust around the young Sun. Tiny particles collided, stuck together and accumulated into larger bodies through gravity and repeated impacts.

Solar System science ↗
◌

Rocky planets

Mercury, Venus, Earth and Mars have solid surfaces. Their differences in atmosphere, geology, magnetic fields and climate reveal the range of outcomes possible for rocky worlds.

Planet guide ↗
◎

Giant planets

Jupiter and Saturn are gas giants; Uranus and Neptune are ice giants. Their deep interiors, rings, storms and moon systems help researchers test models of planetary formation.

Explore planets ↗
✦

Moons and oceans

Some moons have atmospheres, volcanoes or hidden oceans beneath icy crusts. Europa and Enceladus are especially interesting targets in research on potentially habitable environments.

Explore moons ↗
☄

Asteroids and comets

Asteroids preserve clues about early Solar System materials. Comets contain ice and dust, and can develop a glowing cloud and tails when solar heating releases gas and particles.

Explore small bodies ↗
★

Stars and stellar evolution

Stars form as dense clouds collapse under gravity. A star's mass influences its temperature, brightness, lifetime and final evolutionary state.

Discover stars ↗
⟡

Galaxies

Galaxies contain stars, gas, dust and dark matter bound by gravity. The Milky Way contains our Sun and billions of other stars, alongside a wide range of planetary systems.

Explore galaxies ↗
◉

Black holes

Black holes are regions where gravity is so strong that light cannot escape from within the event horizon. Astronomers study their surroundings and gravitational effects.

Explore black holes ↗
λ

Light and wavelengths

Visible light is only a small part of the electromagnetic spectrum. Infrared, radio waves, ultraviolet and X-rays reveal different temperatures, materials and energetic processes.

Explore the spectrum ↗
⊹

Exoplanets

Exoplanets orbit stars beyond our Sun. Researchers discover them by measuring changes in starlight, stellar motion or directly captured light, then investigate their environments.

Explore other worlds ↗
∞

Distance and cosmic time

Light takes time to travel. Looking at distant objects therefore lets us see them as they were in the past, making astronomy a way to investigate the universe's history.

Explore the universe ↗

05 / SPHEREx investigation lab

One sky. Different wavelengths.

SPHEREx studies the sky in 102 infrared spectral bands. Each wavelength can reveal different features and information about cosmic material.

0.98 µm · OTHER VIEW 3.29 µm · DUST

Image credit: NASA/JPL-Caltech. Original image explanation ↗

How to interpret this

What can wavelengths reveal?

SPHEREx observed this region of the Large Magellanic Cloud at different infrared wavelengths. A dust feature is visible in the 3.29-micron image but not in the 0.98-micron view.

Why compare?

Molecules can emit and absorb light at particular wavelengths. Spectral information can help researchers investigate chemical composition and the physical conditions of material in space.

What the slider does

It reveals two published images side by side. It does not align raw exposures, calibrate telescope data or calculate an automated detection score.

What discovery requires

A visual difference alone does not prove a new planet or another discovery. Scientific analysis needs appropriate data, quality checks, context and review.

06 / Scientific practice

Curiosity, evidence and uncertainty.

The goal is not to label every unusual image a discovery. It is to ask testable questions and follow the evidence.

01

Observe

Describe what the image or measurement actually shows, without adding an unsupported explanation.

02

Compare

Review wavelength, exposure, alignment, processing and data quality before interpreting differences.

03

Investigate

Check alternative explanations, source records and scientific context before making a claim.

The solar system and star map on this website are educational models. The SPHEREx slider is a visual aid, not an automated scientific discovery system.
